# Economy spine — how profitable cities actually stay green

Source: Erdgeist profitable-city run, YoyoYoshi GameFAQs 1.1, Economy 2.0 diaries. Filed 2026-09-17.

## The surplus engine

Local extractors (farm, stone, forestry, aggregate) cheapen goods. Cheaper goods → buildings level up → tax rises. That is the only reliable path from village to green budget. Do not skip to downtown hoping for office money.

## What works

- Start tiny, expand in small batches. Grid layouts, compact roads early.
- Essential services only: small clinic, small school, small dump, water, power. No hospital, no university, no landmark.
- Zone residential in staggered batches across different areas to avoid a synchronized death wave.
- Taxes: keep R/C/I around 10–12%; office 8–9%. Above ~12% can collect *less*, not more.
- Build cemetery when deaths start, not before — but have the slot ready.
- Match education to the workforce: early city = industry/commercial jobs, not a wall of offices.
- "Lack of labor" icon on a shop = not enough workers at that education level. Zone matching jobs, do not just add housing.

## What fails

- Mega-zoning one residential wave → everyone arrives young → everyone dies together → health/deathcare overload → spiral.
- Cutting healthcare/deathcare sliders to "save" money → backfires, happiness and XP drop.
- Importing megawatts silently drains the treasury; make or buy with eyes open.
- A self-sufficient city is not possible even manufacturing everything — outside connections and trade remain part of the economy. Do not plan for zero imports.
- Freight policies that double production also double freight burden — only enact when the road network can carry it.

## Council rule

Mayor's first question each tick: is the production panel showing a deficit? If yes, zone the missing extractor before any new housing. Profit follows production, not the other way around.
